Your compass is fibbing. Just a little.

The engine, the electronics, even the tins of beans in the locker all tug at your boat's compass. OmniRose turns a few simple readings into a clear deviation table and a conversion rose, so you know exactly which way you're really heading.

How it works

1Swing your compass

Point the boat at a few known headings and note how far the compass is off each time. It takes about an hour on a calm day. Here's how.

2Type in your readings

OmniRose fits a smooth curve through them and shows you the result straight away, with a nudge if a reading looks odd.

3Print, laminate, done

Download your deviation table and conversion roses as PDFs. Each one has a QR code that brings your readings back whenever you need a reprint.

What you get

A deviation table

The classic card for the chart table: your compass's error on every heading, drawn as a curve so you can read it at a glance. There's also a combined table with compass headings on the other side, which saves you a second round of correction.

A conversion rose

Three rings, one each for true, magnetic and compass, lined up so you can convert any heading just by reading straight across. No sums, no "add east or was it west?" at 3am. See how to use it.

Want to steer 045° true?

Find 45 on the green ring and read across: that's 047° magnetic and 050° on Saucy Sue's compass. Done.
