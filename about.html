About OmniRose

OmniRose makes conversion roses: true, magnetic and your own compass on one card. It's free and open source, for anyone who finds it useful.

It was first written in 2015 as a Python and Django website, then set aside unfinished. In 2026 the code was handed to Claude, Anthropic's AI, to polish up and turn into the free website you're using now, with the original author checking each change along the way.

Your data stays with you

  • Everything happens in your browser. Your readings are never sent to a server.
  • Your readings live in the page's link, after the "#", which browsers don't send to the website. That's why bookmarking the page saves your table.
  • A copy of your latest readings is also kept in your browser's own storage, so following a link away from the tool and coming back doesn't lose them. It stays on your device; "Start afresh" clears it.
  • No accounts, no cookies, no analytics, no adverts.

How it works

For the curious: compass deviation follows a well-known pattern. On a heading θ it can be written as

deviation = A + B·sin θ + C·cos θ + D·sin 2θ + E·cos 2θ + …

The five coefficients A to E are the ones compass adjusters have used for over a century. Roughly: A is a constant offset (often a misaligned lubber line), B and C come from permanent magnetism in the boat, and D and E from soft iron.

OmniRose fits this series to your readings by least squares. It picks how many terms to use with the Bayesian information criterion, a standard way to choose the simplest curve that explains the data, up to seven terms (one harmonic beyond A to E). You can override it under "Advanced" on the tool page. Then it draws the table and the rose straight to PDF in your browser, using the built-in Helvetica font so nothing needs downloading.

Each printout carries a QR code holding a link to your readings, so a laminated card can always find its way back to the tool.

Also in OmniRose

  • Swing by the sun (experimental, feedback welcome): a printable sun pelorus and a drinks can are all you need to measure your deviation. The sun's position comes from NOAA's solar position algorithm.
  • "Where are you sailing?": on the tool page, pick your cruising area and OmniRose works out which variation pages to print, using the World Magnetic Model.

Help make it better

The code is on GitHub under the MIT licence. Bug reports, ideas and fixes are all welcome:

  • Something looks wrong on your table? Open an issue and include the link from the "Keep it" box, so we can see exactly what you see.
  • The site is plain HTML and JavaScript with no build step. Clone it, run a local web server, and open it in your browser. The README has the details.
