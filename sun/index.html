Swing your compass by the sun

No landmarks, no transits, no helpers on the shore: just sunshine, a printout and a drinks can. The sun's position is known exactly for any moment and place, so its shadow tells you which way the boat is really pointing.

1Print the pelorus

A pelorus is a compass card without a magnet. Ours is numbered so that wherever the can's shadow falls, the number under it is the sun's bearing from the bow.

Print at actual size (100%). The check bar should measure 100 mm.

You'll also need

  • A drinks can (an ordinary 330 ml one, not dented).
  • Something flat and stiff to mount the card on, such as a clipboard, plus tape.
  • Your phone, which keeps accurate time and knows where you are.
  • A sunny day with calm water. Mornings and afternoons are best; around midday the shadow is short and the sun moves fast.

Pelorus preview

2Set it up

  1. Mount the card level somewhere in full sun, clear of the shadows of the mast, rigging and sprayhood. On the coachroof or a cockpit seat works well. Tilting the card throws the reading off, especially when the sun is high.
  2. Line up the BOW line exactly parallel to the boat's centreline, pointing forwards. Sight along it to the forestay or the middle of the bow, or use a straight edge against a fore-and-aft bulkhead. Every degree of misalignment is a degree of error on every reading.
  3. Stand the can on the circle that fits it.
  4. Steer a steady heading and wait for the compass to settle. Then, at the same moment, note the time, the compass heading, and where the shadow crosses the scale.
  5. Read both edges of the shadow where they cross the outer scale (or the inner one if the shadow is short), and enter them like 112/124. OmniRose averages them, which is more accurate than guessing the middle.
  6. Turn to the next heading and repeat, all the way round: every 30° or 45° is plenty.

Tip: use the "Now" button as you read the compass. It stamps the time from your phone's clock, which is accurate to the second.

3Where and when

A rough position is fine: a mile or two makes no real difference.

Enter your position and date to work it out, or type it in from your chart.
Advanced: pelorus correction

If you know the BOW line isn't quite fore-and-aft (say from a reading on a known heading, alongside a charted quay), enter the degrees to add to every shadow reading.
